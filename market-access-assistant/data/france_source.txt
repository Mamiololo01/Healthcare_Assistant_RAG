HAS Reimbursement Summary - Connected Medical Device (Synthetic Example)
Product: CardioLink Connect (fictional connected cardiac monitoring device)
Indication: Remote monitoring of adults with chronic heart failure (NYHA class II-III)
Body: Haute Autorite de Sante (HAS), Commission Nationale d'Evaluation des Dispositifs Medicaux
et des Technologies de Sante (CNEDiMTS)

1. Background
CardioLink Connect is a wearable patch device that continuously monitors heart rate, activity, and
thoracic fluid levels, transmitting data to a clinician dashboard for remote monitoring of chronic
heart failure patients. The manufacturer applied for inclusion on the list of reimbursable products
and services (Liste des Produits et Prestations Remboursables, LPPR).

2. Clinical and Organisational Evidence
The submission included a prospective cohort study of 180 patients showing a reduction in
unplanned heart-failure-related hospital admissions compared with standard outpatient follow-up.
CNEDiMTS considered the clinical benefit (Service Attendu) to be established, but noted several
areas of uncertainty that affect the terms of reimbursement.

3. Reasons Reimbursement May Be Conditional
- The cohort study was single-arm and non-randomised, so the magnitude of benefit compared with
  usual remote monitoring pathways already in use in some regions remains uncertain.
- Long-term durability of the reduction in hospital admissions beyond the 12-month study period
  has not been demonstrated, so continued reimbursement may be linked to further data collection.
- The economic evaluation relied on assumptions about avoided hospitalisation costs that CNEDiMTS
  considered only partially validated for the French healthcare setting.
- CNEDiMTS proposed that reimbursement be granted on a conditional basis, tied to real-world data
  collection through a national registry, with a formal re-assessment after two years.

4. Data Privacy Concerns
- The device continuously transmits physiological data to a cloud platform operated by a
  third-party vendor, raising questions about data residency and compliance with French and EU
  data protection requirements (RGPD/GDPR).
- CNEDiMTS asked for clarification on how patient consent is obtained for secondary use of
  monitoring data for product improvement purposes, and whether patients can opt out of such use
  while continuing to use the device for clinical monitoring.
- The dossier did not fully specify data retention periods or the process for deleting patient
  data after a patient stops using the device, which CNEDiMTS flagged as needing clarification
  before full reimbursement terms are finalised.
- Questions were also raised about which entity is the data controller under GDPR when data flows
  between the device manufacturer, the cloud host, and the treating hospital.

5. Recommended Additional Evidence
CNEDiMTS recommended a randomised or comparative study against existing remote monitoring
pathways, longer-term follow-up data on hospitalisation outcomes, and a clearer data governance
framework addressing consent, retention, and controller responsibilities under GDPR, to support a
non-conditional reimbursement decision at the next review.
