UK NICE Technology Appraisal Summary (Synthetic Example)
Product: Oncozalimab (fictional monoclonal antibody)
Indication: Second-line treatment of advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) in adults whose
disease has progressed after platinum-based chemotherapy.
Appraisal type: Single Technology Appraisal (STA)
Committee: NICE Technology Appraisal Committee

1. Background
Oncozalimab is a monoclonal antibody targeting PD-L1, administered by intravenous infusion every
three weeks. The company submitted clinical evidence from a single open-label, randomised phase III
trial (ONCZ-301) comparing oncozalimab against docetaxel in 412 patients with advanced NSCLC.

2. Clinical Evidence Summary
The trial met its primary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS), with a median PFS of 5.8 months
for oncozalimab versus 3.9 months for docetaxel (hazard ratio 0.71, 95% CI 0.58-0.87). However, at
the time of the submission, overall survival (OS) data were immature, with only 38% of the required
death events observed. The committee noted that the immature overall survival data made it difficult
to assess the true long-term survival benefit, and that the OS curves had not yet separated with
confidence.

3. Evidence Gaps Identified by the Committee
- Immature overall survival data at the time of submission, driven by a low number of observed
  death events relative to the planned analysis.
- Uncertainty around the durability of treatment benefit beyond the 12-month follow-up window
  reported in the trial, since long-term data on response duration were not yet available.
- Limited real-world evidence in NHS clinical practice, as the pivotal trial population was drawn
  from a multinational trial that under-represented UK patients and NHS treatment pathways.
- The comparator arm used standard-dose docetaxel, but the committee noted that some UK clinicians
  now use alternative second-line regimens, raising questions about the generalisability of the
  comparison.
- Health-related quality-of-life data were collected but had a high rate of missingness at later
  time points, limiting confidence in the quality-adjusted life year (QALY) estimates.

4. Committee Conclusions
The committee considered that oncozalimab demonstrated a clinically meaningful improvement in
progression-free survival, but concluded that the overall survival benefit could not yet be
confirmed with confidence due to data immaturity. The committee requested that the company provide
updated overall survival data and commit to a managed access arrangement with mandatory registry
data collection through the National Cancer Registration and Analysis Service (NCRAS) to address
the real-world evidence gap. Conditional recommendation was considered subject to a commercial
access agreement and a data collection agreement to resolve the survival uncertainty over the
following 24 months.

5. Recommended Further Evidence
The committee recommended that the company collect mature overall survival data, additional
real-world evidence via a UK-based registry, and updated quality-of-life data with reduced
missingness, to be reviewed at the next scheduled evidence update.
